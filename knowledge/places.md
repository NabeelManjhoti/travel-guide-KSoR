This document is the record's guide to the best places to visit on a budget.
Every figure below is **per person, per day, on the ground** — accommodation,
food and transport within the destination, not the cost of getting there — and
is copied verbatim from the source named beside it.

## How to read these budgets

The budgets below are, in AirHub's words, "a realistic range for someone
traveling smart, not a survival minimum and not a splurge", and "each figure
is per person, per day, on the ground".[^airhub-dest] They are approximate and
move with season and travel style, and they were published in 2025–2026
sources — this document has a `stale_after` date because prices do not stand
still.

## Vietnam

**Daily budget:** Going puts backpacking at **$30–$50 a day**, and a
"comfortable mid-range trip" at $80–$150 a day.[^going-dest] AirHub lists
**$25–$35 a day**.[^airhub-dest] The two sources disagree — both figures are
recorded here, and the record does not choose between them.

**Best time to go:** "May–June or September–October, the shoulder months before
and after the July–September monsoon rains, when hotel and flight prices sit
well below the November–March high season."[^going-dest]

**Note:** "Costs differ by region. Hue and the Mekong Delta run cheaper than Da
Nang or Phu Quoc."[^going-dest]

## Thailand

**Daily budget:** ~**$30–45/day** per AirHub, "great for solo travelers", with
the famous islands drifting toward Western prices in high season — "the fix is
to head north."[^airhub-dest] VoyeGlobal lists **~US$20–35/day** for budget
travelers, "can be lower in northern towns".[^voye-dest]

**Best time to go:** Nov–Feb.[^airhub-dest]

## Cambodia

**Daily budget:** ~**$25–40/day**, "Angkor's temples and easygoing river
towns".[^airhub-dest]

**Note — the Angkor pass:** a single-day pass "costs about $37", and "the
three-day pass slashes the per-day cost".[^airhub-dest] Intrepid names the same
structure as "$37/$62/$72 for one/three/seven days" and recommends the 3-day
pass.[^intrepid-dest]

## Laos

**Daily budget:** the cheapest country of 131 studied by price-comparison site
HelloSafe — a daily spend "could be as little as £12.30 (that's €14.60 or
$16)".[^timeout-dest] Laos is "the only landlocked nation in Southeast Asia,
nestled between Myanmar, China, Thailand, Cambodia and Vietnam".[^timeout-dest]

**Note from the same study:** the figure is heavy on the "could" — the study
spelled out that daily spend could be that low.[^timeout-dest]

## Indonesia

**Daily budget:** roughly **$25–$50 a day** "for hostels, street food, and
public transit".[^going-dest]

**Best time to go:** "April–May or September–October, after the July–August
peak-season rush but before the November–March wet season sets in across Java,
Lombok, and Flores."[^going-dest]

## Nepal

**Daily budget:** ~**US$15–30/day** on a budget; "trekking costs vary depending
on permits/guide/porters but basic food and lodges on trails are
affordable".[^voye-dest]

**Money-saving tips:** "choose tea-house treks instead of fully-supported
guided packages, eat dal bhat for cheap hearty meals, and travel in shoulder
seasons for lower prices. Permits (like TIMS/Annapurna) are fixed costs —
factor them into your plan."[^voye-dest]

## Albania

**Daily budget:** "one of Europe's cheapest countries to visit right now, at
roughly **$30 a day**".[^going-dest] A per-day breakdown: accommodation
**$10–15 per night in a hostel**, meals **$5–10 per day** (some hostels provide
breakfast), a 1 GB data SIM **$10 for 7 days**.[^roam-dest]

**Note:** "a time warp country that offers a unique look into Europe from many
decades ago" — budget and under the radar.[^roam-dest]

## Portugal

**Daily budget:** "the best-value pick in Western Europe, cheaper than Spain,
France, or Italy, with backpackers managing **$55–$70 a day** for hostels,
bakery meals, and public transit" — and "Porto undercuts Lisbon on both hotels
and food".[^going-dest] A place this costed is the reminder that *cheap* means
relative: budget travel is not one price level for every country.
