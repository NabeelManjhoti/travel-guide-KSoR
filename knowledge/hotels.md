This document is the record's guide to cheap hotels. The boundary is
deliberate: **cheap** hotels. Luxury hotels and premium stays are out of scope,
because they clash with the budget positioning. Everything below is a method
that a published source states, cited to that source.

## Compare, then book direct

Start with a metasearch to see the field, then check the hotel's own price.

- MoneySavingExpert's comparison starters: "TripAdvisor" (for its traveller
  ratings), and "Trivago, HotelsCombined, TravelSupermarket, Hotels.com" for
  breadth.[^mse-hot]
- "Booking websites typically cream off between 13 and 25 per cent in
  commission, so it will nearly always be cheaper to book through the hotel's
  website."[^times-hot]
- "Some hotel chains generally pay a fee for bookings that come through a
  third-party comparison service, so chances are they might undercut the best
  quote to avoid the fee."[^budgetdirect-hot]
- NerdWallet: "don't neglect a hotel's own website. Some chains promise the
  best rate when you book via their site."[^nerdwallet-hot]

Then call. "It's better to call the hotel than email"[^times-hot] — "tell the
hotel the price you found via other sites and see if they can beat it."[^nerdwallet-hot]
When booking, "try asking for the 'cheapest non-refundable rate' to potentially
get to the accommodation's lowest rate before negotiating even further."[^budgetdirect-hot]

## The free-cancellation safety-net

Book early with free cancellation, then keep hunting and rebook if the price
drops — "once your dates are fixed, make a booking that will give you a full
refund without penalty; like many sites, booking.com allows you to cancel this
way, usually up to 24 hours before check-in."[^times-hot] "Regardless of how
you've booked the hotel, you can usually cancel for free up to a certain point
– typically 24 to 48 hours before your stay starts."[^mse-hot]

The same mechanism works the other way: compare the non-refundable rate against
the free-cancellation rate — "many sites, even if they have a 'free
cancellation' option, offer even deeper discounts to those booking rooms with
non-refundable rates."[^going-hot]

## Price guarantees and trackers

- Some sites guarantee to refund the difference if the price drops. Booking.com
  "will grant a refund of the difference up to 24 hours before check-in — look
  for 'Found this room cheaper elsewhere?' on your confirmation page".[^mse-hot]
- Free price-trackers "Rebookey, Hotel Price Track and Rebookr claim they're
  able to find you a cheaper rate on a room you've already booked, so long as
  it has free cancellation", then alert you and "guide you through cancelling
  and rebooking yourself".[^mse-hot]

## When to book

- "The best time to book a hotel is around 30 days prior to your stay" — but
  "booking 7-10 days before you plan to arrive should turn up more affordable
  results" for a last-minute trip (Cheryl Rosner, co-founder of Stayful).[^standard-hot]
- In city hotels, "Monday nights nearly always have the cheapest rates."[^times-hot]

## Which days of the week

- "If you can, start your hotel stay on a Tuesday. It's 'the best day of the
  week for saving money." For weekends, "include a Sunday night stay... there
  are usually more cheaper hotel rooms available on Sunday nights."[^standard-hot]
- Budget Direct on the same axis: "booking during the week, or specifically on
  a Sunday, could potentially give you the same hotel room for cheaper."[^budgetdirect-hot]
- "Travel off-season"[^standard-hot] and "avoid staying during big events" —
  "hotels hike their prices up when conferences and conventions are happening
  nearby."[^standard-hot]

## Last-minute apps

"Apps like HotelTonight and One:Night specialize in helping last-minute
travelers get cheap hotel rooms."[^nerdwallet-hot] The trade-off: "last-minute
bookings don't work in every circumstance, though. Traveling for a crowded
conference or holiday can lead to a spike in demand — and price."[^nerdwallet-hot]

## Location and room type

- "Staying in the heart of the city typically costs more. Save a few bucks by
  staying away from the city center, but near public transportation" — NerdWallet's
  example: the Hyatt Regency O'Hare at **$152 per night**, "just a few minutes'
  walk from the Chicago Transit Authority's Blue Line".[^nerdwallet-hot]
- "Hostels are a great option for budget travelers... Most hostels offer a
  handful of private rooms with en suite bathrooms."[^nerdwallet-hot]

## Bundling

"It's also worth looking at a bundled deal (hotel + flight, or hotel + car)
from a site like Expedia, as these can sometimes save you money" — but "check
the hotel's site for other potential perks you won't find on an aggregator—
like free WiFi, parking, or breakfast."[^going-hot]
