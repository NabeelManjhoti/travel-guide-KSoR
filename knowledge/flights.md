This document is the record's guide to cheap flights. Two boundaries are
deliberate: **cheap** flights — business-class and premium-deal guidance is out
of scope — and the record points at **how to find** cheap flights; it does not
book or buy tickets on anyone's behalf. Everything below is a method stated by
a published source, cited to that source.

## The two search engines' own tools

**Skyscanner** names four tools for its price work, and describes itself as
scanning "all the top airlines and travel providers across the net" so you
"compare flight fares and other travel costs in one place":[^skyscanner-flights]

1. **Whole Month search** — "find the cheapest dates to travel" when your
   month is fixed; "a full breakdown of daily fares within any month you
   choose."[^skyscanner-flights][^gf-date-flights]
2. **Cheapest Month** — "identifies the month with the lowest 'from' prices
   based on recent search data", for when "you don't know when to go, only that
   you want the best deal". Prices shown are estimates "based on what other
   travellers found on Skyscanner in the last eight days".[^gf-date-flights]
3. **Explore 'Everywhere'** — "the cheapest flights from your departure city to
   anywhere in the world", useful for discovering cities as much as comparing
   fares. Skyscanner's own examples include flights to Iceland "as little as
   $208" and to Puerto Rico "under $100".[^skyscanner-flights]
4. **Price Alerts** — "we'll alert you when we detect a change in the flight
   price", and "you can set a Price Alert for multiple destinations at
   once".[^skyscanner-flights]

It also covers **last-minute deals**: "it's possible to snag tickets days or
even hours before a flight for less than you would have if you bought tickets
far ahead of time."[^skyscanner-flights]

**Google Flights** separates results into a "Best" tab and a "Cheapest" tab:
"the 'Cheapest' tab prioritizes low prices, and includes additional itineraries
and prices from online travel agents", with trade-offs such as "self-transfer
of the luggage between connecting flights" and "fly through different airports
in the same city on the onward and return flights."[^gf-support-flights]

Google Flights also shows three **booking-nudge tips**:

- "Prices are unlikely to drop before you book" — shown when it "predicts with
  a high degree of confidence that prices for the trip will not drop."
- "Prices are less than usual" — "when prices for the trip are significantly
  less than expected based on an analysis of past prices of similar trips."
- "Prices are likely to increase" — when it "is able to predict with a high
  degree of confidence that prices will increase and by approximately what
  amount."[^gf-support-flights]

## When to book

- Google's own data point: on average, "cheapest Christmas season flights tend
  to be available **71 days before departure**", with the typical lowest-price
  range for mid-December trips roughly **54 to 78 days before departure**.[^cntraveler-flights]
  The same source warns the window "can vary depending on how specific or
  complicated your plans might be".[^cntraveler-flights]
- Google Travel's general guidance: "it's usually best to book at least a few
  weeks in advance for domestic flights and a few months in advance for cheap
  international flights."[^gftp-flights]
- Going's general rule of thumb: "usually the best bet to get a great deal on
  airfare is to book early."[^going-flights]

## Fly into a cheaper hub, then hop

"The way the math works out for many budget travelers is getting a cheap fare
to Europe from your home airport, even if it's not where you actually want to
be—and then hopping on a budget airline (or a train or bus ride) to get from
your entry point to your vacation spot."[^going-flights]
